Why Early Fault Alerts Protect Uptime

Why catching a fault early is worth more to a small station than the repair itself.

No redundancy at a small station

A small station typically runs on just one or two dispensers, with no backup. An unplanned fault that takes a dispenser offline in the middle of the day is a direct, immediate hit to that day's revenue — not just a maintenance inconvenience to schedule around later.

Turning an emergency into a scheduled task

Because the AI layer flags a developing fault — a worn seal, a pressure anomaly — before it becomes a hard failure, an owner can schedule the fix during a genuinely low-traffic window, early morning or late night, instead of losing peak-hour throughput to an emergency repair.

Same mechanism, different vantage point

This is the identical anomaly-detection process described on the AI detection page — the difference here is purely which side of the outcome you're looking at: the technical mechanism, versus the operational upside it produces for the person running the station.
