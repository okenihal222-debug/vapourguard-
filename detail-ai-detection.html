How The Anomaly Detection Actually Works

How an on-device model learns what 'normal' looks like at one specific station, and catches the moment something drifts away from it.

Learning a station's own baseline

The edge AI model doesn't start with a fixed, one-size-fits-all rulebook. Over its first weeks of operation, it baselines each individual station's own normal recovery pattern: how much vapor a typical tank-fill or refuelling event returns, at what pressure, over what time window — because a busy highway station and a quiet rural one have genuinely different “normal.”

Catching drift before it's a leak

Once that baseline exists, the model watches for deviations. A coupling that suddenly returns less vapor than its own history predicts is a strong, early signal of a worn seal or a developing fault — often well before it's large enough for a person to notice or smell.

Why it runs on the device itself

Inference runs on-device rather than depending on a constant cloud connection, so the fault-detection function keeps working even during the patchy connectivity many rural sites actually have. That's a deliberate design choice made for the environment this device is actually going into, not an engineering afterthought.

How an alert reaches the owner

When an anomaly is flagged, it surfaces two ways: instantly through the enclosure's own status LED for anyone standing nearby, and remotely through an SMS/app alert straight to the owner's phone. See the Live Dashboard for exactly what that alert looks like when it arrives.
