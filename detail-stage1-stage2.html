Why Combining Both Stages Into One Retrofit Matters

Why treating two separate capture stages as one retrofit is the single decision that makes a small-station price point possible.

Two different moments, two different leaks

A fuel station loses vapor at two distinct moments: Stage 1, when a tanker fills the underground storage tank, and Stage 2, when a customer's vehicle is refuelled at the dispenser. Conventional vapor recovery treats these as two separate systems, because historically they were engineered, sold, and installed as two separate systems.

Why that's a problem for small stations

Two separate installations means two footprints, two sets of plumbing, two installation visits, and roughly double the hardware cost. That's a manageable ask for a high-throughput city station with capital to spare. For a small rural outlet running on thin margins, it's a non-starter — which is exactly why Stage 2 systems in particular have stayed an almost entirely urban phenomenon in India.

The retrofit design decision

VaporGuard's unit integrates both capture stages into a single compact enclosure, sharing sensors, valves, and the monitoring controller between them instead of duplicating hardware for each stage. That single decision is what brings the assumed hardware cost down to a level a small station can realistically consider. ASSUMPTION — ₹2,00,000

What this means day to day

Practically: one installation visit, one enclosure to maintain, and one dashboard — not two separate systems for an owner or attendant to think about.
