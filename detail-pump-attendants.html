The Problem — Deep Dive

Pump Attendants: Chronic, Everyday Exposure

Why the people standing at the nozzle all day carry more risk than almost anyone else at a fuel station — and why small, rural stations carry the most of it.

A job spent standing in vapor

Attendants at Indian fuel stations spend 8–12 hour shifts standing at the exact point where fuel vapor is released — twice over. Once at the nozzle during every single refuelling event, and again near the tank vents whenever a tanker unloads. Unlike a customer who's there for two minutes, an attendant repeats that exposure dozens or hundreds of times a shift, day after day, for years.

What the studies actually found

Benzene is a volatile component of petrol vapor and a recognised human carcinogen — that's established toxicology, not a marketing claim. Multiple peer-reviewed Indian studies have compared long-serving pump attendants against unexposed control groups and consistently found measurably altered blood and hematology markers — a biological signature consistent with chronic low-level benzene exposure, not incidental discomfort. VERIFIED — published studies

Why small and rural stations carry more of this risk

Stage 2 nozzle vapor recovery — the hardware that captures vapor right at the point a customer's tank is being filled — has been standard at urban, high-throughput stations for years. It's the roughly 29% of India's 100,000+ stations that are rural, plus many more small urban outlets, that have never had access to it, simply because existing systems were priced and engineered for large stations. VERIFIED — Business Standard, Dec 2025
